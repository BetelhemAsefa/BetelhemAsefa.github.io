Xbox Game Platform Database & SQL Analysis

  • MySQL
  • SQL
  • Python
  • Database design
Question
Which games and DLC drive revenue, how concentrated is player spending, and how often do buyers of a game also buy its DLC?
Approach
Designed an 11-table relational database from an EER diagram, then wrote 12 analytical queries using joins, CTEs, window functions, and CASE logic. Added reporting views, stored procedures, triggers, and role-based access, and charted the results in Python (pandas, matplotlib, seaborn).
Result
Built KPIs for revenue, average order value, spend tiers (Bronze to Platinum), DLC attach rate, and subscription adoption by region. Note: the database holds a small sample (about 25 rows per table), so the numbers illustrate the method rather than real-world findings.

Predicting Customer Response to Marketing Campaigns

  • R
  • Classification
  • Imbalanced data
  • Model evaluation
Question
Which customers are most likely to respond to a marketing campaign, so the company can target them instead of everyone?
Approach
Cleaned a Kaggle marketing dataset of customer demographics, spending, and past campaign responses, and found that only about 15% of customers responded. Trained logistic regression, random forest, and a neural network (tuned with 10-fold cross-validation) on a 70/30 train/test split, and compared them on accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, F1, and Kappa.
Result
All three models scored 88–89% accuracy, but that number was misleading: a model can look accurate just by predicting "no response" for almost everyone. The neural network was best at finding actual responders (51.5% vs. 31.3% for random forest) and had the highest Kappa (0.51), so I chose it for targeting campaigns.

Customer Segmentation with K-Means Clustering

  • R
  • K-means
  • Customer analytics
Question
Can customers be grouped by income and spending so marketing can be tailored to each group?
Approach
Explored 200 mall customers, then ran k-means on standardized age, annual income, and spending score. Both the elbow method and silhouette analysis pointed to five clusters.
Result
Found five segments and gave each a marketing action. The biggest opportunity is the high-income, low-spending group (about $86K income, spending score of 19): they can afford more but aren't buying, so they're the best target for promotions and personalized recommendations. High-income high spenders (score of 81) should be kept with loyalty programs, and the low-income, low-spending group warrants less marketing spend.

Hate Speech Detection with Text Analytics

  • R
  • NLP
  • Sentiment analysis
  • Topic modeling
Question
What patterns separate harmful from non-harmful language online?
Approach
The dataset had 440,000+ labeled posts (about 82% non-hate), too big for R's memory limit, so I drew a balanced sample of 6,000 posts (3,000 per class). I cleaned and tokenized the text, compared the groups with TF-IDF, sentiment and emotion lexicons, LDA topic modeling, and bigrams/trigrams, and trained a Naive Bayes classifier.
Result
Hate speech used a narrower, more offensive vocabulary, was much more negative (especially anger and disgust), and clustered around three themes: racial targeting, gender and sexual harassment, and political extremism. Non-hate posts covered a far wider range of topics. Limits: a bag-of-words model can't catch sarcasm or coded language, so a transformer model like BERT is the next step.
